# Souls & Reliquaries

## Reliquaries
- Reliquaries can hold up to 3 souls, and can have up to 3 modifiers on them. You start the campaign with an empty, mundane reliquary (no mods, no souls). 
You can only equip one reliquary. 
- You can add random mods to your reliquary, up to 3, with a _Shrine of Annexation_. 
If a reliquary already has 3 mods, this shrine will instead upgrade a random mod on the reliquary.
- You can replace mods on a reliquary with a _Shrine of Refinement_.
If possible, the new mod will be upgraded to match the investment made in the removed mod (if possible).
- A reliquary's mods will often be conditional on, scale with, or impact its contained souls. It can be valuable to find synergies 
between your souls and your reliquary, and to use Shrines of _Soul Transference_ and _Temporal Shifting_ to optimise them.

## Souls
- Souls have two modifiers:
  1. A passive mod, which is always active
  2. A proc mod, which might cause an effect to go off, or apply a bonus for a short duration.
- Each soul also has an _origin_ and an _era_. These have no effect on their own, but can interact with reliquary mods.
  - Note that while the pairs of passive and proc mods are predefined, the origin and era of a soul are randomly generated and can be changed with shrines. 
- By default, a soul has a 10% chance to trigger its proc mod at the start of each round of combat. This can be increased
via a _Shrine of Manifestation_, or via mods on your reliquary.
- As souls' mods provide highly targetable buffs to your character, upgrading them (via _Shrines of Fulfilment_) faces some diminishing returns not
present in other loot types.
  - More specifically, on each of the first two times you upgrade a mod on your soul,
the future point cost for upgrading it increases by 1.
